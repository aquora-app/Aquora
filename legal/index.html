2. Description of Service

Aquora is a screen time management and mindfulness application that helps users build healthier digital habits. Features include:

  • App Limits & Group Management — group apps and configure daily/session limits
  • Screen Time Tracking — usage statistics and session history
  • Unlocking Quiz Challenges — answer quiz questions across 19 categories to unlock blocked apps (Premium adds an AI-generated question bank)
  • Meditation Sessions — guided breathing, focus, body scan, gratitude, and manifestation practices
  • Ambient Sounds — selectable nature and noise sounds for meditation
  • Journal & Manifestation — personal entries with goals and mood tracking
  • Personality Archetype System — 4-axis personality assessment with monthly reports
  • AI Reminders — scheduled and in-the-moment personalized reminders based on your behavior patterns
  • No-Unlocking Challenges — commit to a 30-day challenge of not unlocking selected apps (requires Strict Mode)
  • Achievements & Rewards — badge system for streaks, time saved, and consistent practice
  • Token System — earn virtual tokens through challenges, redeemable for premium access
  • Referral Program — invite friends with codes for mutual rewards
  • Shareable Cards — export personality and achievement cards as images
  • Tip Jar — optional tips to support development
  • Daily Scroll Forecast — a market-level index built from public data about how noisy and heavy the internet feels today, presented like a weather forecast (Starter tier and above)
  • Mood Check-In & Mood Report — an optional, skippable check-in on how you feel, and a report comparing your own curve with the world's (the check-in lives inside the scroll forecast page, so it needs Starter or above; the report needs Premium)
  • Emotional Weather Alerts — an occasional local notification on days when the public mood surges, at most one per day; on Starter and above it can be switched off on its own under AI Reminder Settings, and on any tier you can turn off all Aquora notifications from the Me tab

AI features (quiz question generation, AI reminders) are powered by third-party AI providers — Google Gemini and DeepSeek. Only anonymized, feature-specific data is sent; see the Privacy Policy tab for exactly what each request contains.

Free, Starter, and Premium

Aquora has three access tiers. Higher tiers include everything in the tiers below them.

  • Free — 1 app limit group, 6 quiz categories, 3 guided meditations, 2 ambient sounds (plus Silence), 1 breathing pattern, 5 journal entries, and today's session history.
  • Starter (one-time purchase) — 3 app limit groups, 9 quiz categories (the 6 free ones plus one of your choice in each of the 3 paid question groups, chosen once), all guided meditations, ambient sounds and breathing patterns, the daily scroll forecast, and the full personality reading.
  • Premium (subscription) — unlimited app limit groups, all 19 quiz categories, the AI question bank, AI reminders (daily and in-the-moment), unlimited journal entries and session history, the personal mood report, and monthly evolution reports.

A few caps apply on every tier: meditation history keeps your most recent 100 sessions. Where a limit changed after you already exceeded it, what you already created is kept — the App does not delete groups or entries you set up under an earlier version.

Your archetype code, its name, its rarity badge and the shareable card are free forever; the radar chart and the axis-by-axis reading require Starter or above.

Referral codes are stored in Apple's CloudKit public database for verification. Owner and redeemer identities are stored as anonymized hashes only.

Abuse of the referral system, including self-referrals or fraudulent redemptions, may result in revocation of rewards.

1. Introduction

Your privacy is important to us. This Privacy Policy explains how Aquora collects, uses, and protects your information when you use our App.

2. Information We Collect

Sign In with Apple

If you choose to sign in, we collect your Apple User ID, name, and email address provided by Apple. Your Apple User ID is stored securely in the device Keychain. Your name and email are stored locally on your device only.

Screen Time Data

With your explicit permission, we access device usage data through Apple's Screen Time API and DeviceActivity framework to provide usage tracking, app limiting, and shield features. Raw usage data is processed locally on your device and never uploaded. If you enable AI Reminders (premium), aggregated statistics derived from it (e.g., average daily minutes, peak hours, unlock counts) are included in AI generation requests — see Section 5.

User Preferences & Progress

Settings such as your selected app limits, meditation preferences, ambient sound choices, quiz progress, reward tokens, achievements, and streak data are stored locally using UserDefaults and App Groups.

User-Generated Content

Journal entries, manifestation goals, mood tracking, and meditation notes you create are stored locally on your device. We do not access, read, or transmit this content.

Behavior Logs

To power personalized features (AI Reminders, Personality Archetype, monthly reports), the App stores the following behavior logs locally with a 90-day retention period:

  • Quiz answers (question ID, category, correctness, response time, hour of day, app context)
  • Reflection responses (selected options, app context, timestamp)
  • App unlock events (app group name, unlock method, optional user-stated reason, day/hour)
  • Post-session feeling check-ins (worth it / neutral / regret, with timestamp)

This data stays on your device and is used only for local analysis.

Personality Archetype Data

Your responses to the personality questionnaire and your derived 4-letter archetype code are stored locally. Monthly archetype snapshots are kept for trend tracking. To compute rarity statistics, an anonymized record (random device UUID + your archetype code only) is uploaded to Apple's CloudKit public database. This record contains no identifying information.

Referral Information

When you generate or redeem a referral code, the code along with SHA-256 hashed identifiers is stored in Apple's CloudKit public database to validate redemptions and award rewards. We never store plaintext identifiers in CloudKit.

Subscription Information

Subscription and tip purchase status is managed through Apple's StoreKit framework. We do not have access to your payment information or credit card details.

Photo Library (Optional)

If you choose to save or share personality cards or achievement badges, the App requests permission to write images to your Photo Library. We do not read existing photos.

Scroll Forecast Personalization (All Optional, Device-Only)

The first time you open the scroll forecast, the App offers three optional inputs so the forecast can be phrased for you. Every one of them can be skipped, and every one of them stays on this device: none is ever sent to us, to any AI provider, to CloudKit, or to iCloud, and none is carried over to another device.

  • Year of birth — the year only. We never ask for or store a full date of birth. It is used on-device to place you in an age band so the forecast can show the band's figures, and to apply a more conservative presentation if you are under 18.
  • Nearest city — chosen by you from a short list. The App does not use location services and has never requested location permission; this is a name you pick, not a position we measure. It is used only to pick which city's weather line to show.
  • Your frequent apps — a confirmation of which apps you use most, pre-filled from the limits you already created. It is stored on-device as category shares and display names.

Mood Check-Ins & Mood Report

If you tap through the mood check-in on the forecast page, the App records what you chose (how pleasant, how activated, and an optional category) and combines it over time with your post-session feelings, post-meditation mood, and journal mood into a private estimate of your own emotional pattern. That estimate and the report built from it are computed and stored entirely on this device. They are never included in any network request, never placed in an AI prompt, never written to CloudKit or iCloud, and are not carried over to another device. Deleting your account clears them along with everything else in the App's local storage.

4. Data Storage & Security

The vast majority of user data is stored locally on your device. Data shared between the main app and extensions (Shield, Device Activity Monitor, Shield Action) uses secure App Group containers.

iCloud Key-Value Store

Token balance, subscription status, and transaction history may be synced via Apple's iCloud Key-Value Store to restore your data across devices or after reinstallation. This data is encrypted by Apple's iCloud infrastructure. You can disable iCloud sync in your device settings.

CloudKit Public Database

Four types of records are stored in our CloudKit public database:

  • ReferralCode — referral code string with hashed (SHA-256) owner and redeemer identifiers
  • Redemption — one record per code redemption: the code, hashed (SHA-256) redeemer and owner identifiers, and a timestamp
  • ArchetypeUser — anonymous device UUID paired with your personality code and an update timestamp, used to calculate archetype rarity (rarity is computed from a random sample of up to 400 records)
  • Feedback — when you submit feedback via the in-app form, your message, feedback category, star rating, optional email, app and iOS version, and current archetype code are saved as a CloudKit record. Stored anonymously (no device ID, no user ID); only the developer reads them via the CloudKit Dashboard.

These records contain no personally identifiable information.

Keychain

Your Apple User ID is stored in the device Keychain with encryption and is not accessible to other apps.

5. Third-Party Services

The App uses the following third-party services:

  • Apple Sign In — for authentication (Apple User ID verification)
  • Apple StoreKit 2 — for subscription and tip purchase management
  • Apple Family Controls / DeviceActivity — for screen time management
  • Apple iCloud Key-Value Store — for syncing token and subscription data
  • Apple CloudKit — for referral code validation, anonymous archetype rarity statistics, and storing feedback submissions
  • Google Gemini API — used to generate quiz questions and AI reminder content. Two separate request paths transmit different data: (1) Quiz generation sends only your selected question categories, device language, and recently cached question topics (to avoid duplicates). (2) AI Reminder generation sends a behavior summary derived from your local usage data — including up to your three most-unlocked group names with unlock counts (plus any groups in active no-unlock challenges), recurring habit-window times, average daily screen time and trend, peak-hour and night/morning usage ratios, your most frequent reflection answer choices, your strongest and weakest quiz categories, and your derived personality code — so Gemini can write reminders that reference your actual patterns. Never transmitted to Google: contacts, message contents, photo library contents, location, or any journal / meditation entries.
  • DeepSeek API — an alternative AI provider used for the same two request paths with the same payloads as described for Gemini above. Requests are routed to DeepSeek by default for users on the mainland-China App Store using Chinese, and temporarily for any user while Gemini is unreachable (automatic failover, up to 6 hours). The same never-transmitted list applies.
  • GitHub Pages — hosts the scroll forecast data file. The App downloads a single public JSON file from aquora-app.github.io. This is a plain GET: the request carries no user data, no account identifier, no query parameters and no custom headers, and nothing is uploaded. As with any web request, GitHub's servers see the standard connection information (such as your IP address and user agent) in their own access logs. This request is made on every tier, including Free, because the same file also decides whether an emotional weather alert is due.

One addition to the AI reminder payload described above: the request also carries a short block describing today's public mood climate. Everything in it comes from the published forecast file: two macro figures, a few band labels, and the category codes of the day's active events. Your year of birth, your city, your frequent apps, and anything derived from your mood check-ins are never part of it — and neither is which age band you fall into: that choice is made on the device, used to pick which band's figure to read, and then discarded.

Alerts that fire because you opened an app you had limited are immediate feedback on a limit you set yourself, and are not withheld.

All notifications are generated entirely on your device. We do not use remote push notification servers.
